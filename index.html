Privacy

We do not record what you browse.

Not as a policy we promise to follow. As something our software cannot do — because we removed the parts that could.

  • No record of what you looked at. Scores are cached against the product, never against you. The answer for a given item is the same answer everyone else gets, so there is nothing personal to store in the first place.
  • Your free-check counter can't name a product. It stores a one-way hash instead of the product identifier. That is all it needs — enough to recognise something it has already counted, so you're never charged twice for the same item — and it means the table cannot be turned back into a list of what you viewed. Not by us, not by anyone who obtained a copy.
  • Nothing connects a browser to a person. The extension has an anonymous identifier so we can count how many installs are active. We used to keep a list, on each account, of the browsers that had signed in — which meant the two could be joined. We deleted that list, and a test now fails the build if anyone puts it back.
  • Diagnostics are yours to switch on. We can't. If support needs to see what happened on a page, you turn it on in the extension and read us a code. There is no screen on our side that takes an email address and starts recording — the capability isn't in the software. Sessions expire in 24 hours and are identified by that code, never by you.

The four questions

Four things a typical online product listing doesn't answer.

Anyone can read a listing. We trace the seller of record through corporate filings and trademark registries to the parent company that actually collects the money — and cite every source.

You can't tell who is accountable if something goes wrong. You can't tell whether your money supports an American company or only an American storefront. And you can't tell whether “sold by” means the retailer made any judgment about the product at all.

Every score answers four separate questions about the product. Together they tell a story that no single label — Sold by, Ships from, Manufacturer — can ever tell on its own.

01 Made in

Where the product was manufactured

Not where the seller is. Not where the retailer ships it from. The actual country the unit was built in — the fact most online listings leave out.

02 Ships from

Where the unit originates

A retailer's fulfillment center in Ohio? Direct-from-China drop-ship? Different shipping experiences, different delivery times, different accountability.

03 Retailer

Who's transacting with you

The legal seller-of-record — the retailer itself, an authorized distributor, or a third-party marketplace seller. Affects returns, consumer protection, and counterfeit risk.

04 Money goes to

Where the profit ultimately lands

The brand parent's country — the entity that captures the wholesale margin from every sale. A HORVELL watch on a US storefront: retailer is US, but the money flows to Japan.

How it works

Three steps, mostly invisible.

You shop the way you already do. Everything else happens quietly in the background — and you see the answer inline, right where you were already looking.

You land on a product

Browse the supported retailers as usual (Amazon, Target, Walmart, and 30 other major retailers today, more coming). The extension wakes up the moment you hit a product-detail page — nothing else. No history tracking, no other sites.

Claude AI reads it

Anthropic's frontier model reads the full product page — Country of Origin fields, ships-from text, seller storefront, brand website, reviews — and reasons over it against trademark filings, customs records, corporate registries, and its own trained knowledge of brand-parent chains. Every claim must cite a real source.

You see the answer

Four flag-and-percent indicators appear inline. Every one has a confidence band, a color cue, and a "How we got this" panel with every URL we relied on.

Methodology

Plain-English methodology, in 90 seconds.

What we read from the page

Field names below reflect Amazon. The same signal categories apply to every retailer we support — 33 today across general, hardware, furniture, electronics, warehouse, fashion, beauty, and auto parts. See the full list.

  • Country of Origin field from the retailer's structured product details — when present, this is our strongest signal.
  • Ships-from and Sold-by lines from the buy box.
  • Seller storefront — the "Detailed Seller Information" page where the legal entity name and registered country actually live.
  • Brand storefront and website — cross-checked against RDAP, the global domain registry.
  • Trademark filings from our own USPTO index of 3.8M+ records.
  • Customer reviews and Q&A, scanned for "Made in X" claims and shipping mentions.

What our AI does with it

We send the assembled evidence to Anthropic's Claude, which combines it with trained knowledge of brands and companies. When signals are thin, Claude runs live web searches against business registries, brand websites, LinkedIn, and news — and every URL is cited on the badge.

When we don't know, we say so

Confidence isn't fluff. A 50% / 20% confidence score means we genuinely cannot tell. We'd rather be honest than wrong-but-confident.

When we're wrong

Tell us. We'll fix the score for everyone.

Every score has a Contest this finding button. Tell us what you know — your name, your relationship to the product, what you've seen. We verify your email and your report enters our review queue.

When our team validates a correction, the AI score is replaced with a manually-verified one — visible to all future users with a ✓ Verified mark.

It isn't a complaint box. It's a correction mechanism. The whole point is to improve the dataset over time.

Most of the trillion-plus dollars Americans spend online each year flows through products whose origin is not easy to see from the listing. We think you should be able to.

FAQ

Common questions.

Which retailers do you support?

Thirty-three retailers across 32 domains today — covering general retail (Amazon, Target, Walmart), home improvement, home & furniture, electronics, warehouse membership, fashion, beauty, and auto parts. See the full list with categories, or tell us which one to add next.

Is this only about Chinese products?

No — the engine identifies any country. The color of the bar is not our judgement about a country: it is red when the origin appears on the US State Department's published list of governments with a documented "policy or pattern" of human trafficking (Trafficking in Persons Report, 2025), applied uniformly to all thirteen — Afghanistan, Belarus, Burma, Cambodia, China, Cuba, Eritrea, Iran, North Korea, Russia, South Sudan, Sudan and Syria. Everything else reads as neutral. That is a determination about a government, not about any company, brand, or product, and it does not mean a product from those countries was made with forced labour. If the State Department revises the list, our colors change with it.

How accurate are the scores?

It varies by signal strength. When the retailer's listing has a structured "Country of Origin" field, our confidence is highest; when we're relying on brand recognition or web search alone, we say so with a lower confidence percentage. The confidence band on the badge is our best estimate — treat low-confidence scores as flags to look further, not conclusions.

How does Hong Kong / Taiwan get handled?

Hong Kong (HK), Macau (MO), and Taiwan (TW) are scored as their own countries. Never folded into mainland China (CN). This is a hard rule in our system.

Do you collect my browsing history?

No — and we have built it so that we can't. The extension only activates on product pages of the retailers we support, and the only data sent to our backend is the product identifier plus what's visible on that page. We do not collect your retailer account info, your order history, or anything from unsupported sites.

More than that: there is no record anywhere in our systems of the products a particular person has looked at. Scores are cached against the product, not against you — the same answer serves everyone who views that item. Our free-check counter stores a one-way hash rather than the product identifier, so it can tell that it has counted something without being able to say what. And no table maps a browser to a person; the link that would have made that possible has been removed, with automated tests that fail the build if anyone re-introduces it. Full details in our Privacy Policy.

Is this free?

Your first 20 checks are free, no credit card required. They're a one-time allowance, not a monthly one. Beyond that, subscriptions start at $4.99/mo — see the pricing page.

How do I contest a score?

Click the toolbar icon → scroll to Contest this finding → fill in the form. You'll need to provide your name, email, and stated affiliation or credentials. We email you a verification link before the report enters our queue. Verified reports are typically reviewed within a few business days.

I represent a brand — how do I correct a score about my product?

Same path: Contest this finding. We treat first-party brand corrections with high priority. Identify yourself as someone associated with the brand in the "affiliation" field, and provide a corporate email so we can verify the connection.
