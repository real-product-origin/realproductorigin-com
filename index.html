The four questions

Four things a typical online product listing doesn't answer.

Every score answers four separate questions about the product. Together they tell a story that no single label — Sold by, Ships from, Manufacturer — can ever tell on its own.

01 Made in

Where the product was manufactured

Not where the seller is. Not where the retailer ships it from. The actual country the unit was built in — the fact most online listings leave out.

02 Ships from

Where the unit originates

A retailer's fulfillment center in Ohio? Direct-from-China drop-ship? Different shipping experiences, different delivery times, different accountability.

03 Retailer

Who's transacting with you

The legal seller-of-record — the retailer itself, an authorized distributor, or a third-party marketplace seller. Affects returns, consumer protection, and counterfeit risk.

04 Money goes to

Where the profit ultimately lands

The brand parent's country — the entity that captures the wholesale margin from every sale. A Seiko watch on a US storefront: retailer is US, but the money flows to Japan.

How it works

Three steps, mostly invisible.

You shop the way you already do. Everything else happens quietly in the background — and you see the answer inline, right where you were already looking.

You land on a product

Browse the supported retailers as usual (Amazon, Target, and Walmart today, more coming). The extension wakes up the moment you hit a product-detail page — nothing else. No history tracking, no other sites.

We read the page

We extract every visible signal — Country of Origin, ships-from text, seller storefront, brand website, reviews, trademark filings — and cross-reference them against independent sources.

You see the answer

Four flag-and-percent indicators appear inline. Every one has a confidence band, a colour cue, and a "How we got this" panel with every URL we relied on.

Methodology

Plain-English methodology, in 90 seconds.

What we read from the page

Field names below reflect Amazon. Target and Walmart are also supported today, and the same signal categories will apply as we add eBay and Etsy.

  • Country of Origin field from the retailer's structured product details — when present, this is our strongest signal.
  • Ships-from and Sold-by lines from the buy box.
  • Seller storefront — the "Detailed Seller Information" page where the legal entity name and registered country actually live.
  • Brand storefront and website — cross-checked against RDAP, the global domain registry.
  • Trademark filings from our own USPTO index of 3.8M+ records.
  • Customer reviews and Q&A, scanned for "Made in X" claims and shipping mentions.

What our AI does with it

We send the assembled evidence to Anthropic's Claude, which combines it with trained knowledge of brands and companies. When signals are thin, Claude runs live web searches against business registries, brand websites, LinkedIn, and news — and every URL is cited on the badge.

When we don't know, we say so

Confidence isn't fluff. A 50% / 20% confidence score means we genuinely cannot tell. We'd rather be honest than wrong-but-confident.

When we're wrong

Tell us. We'll fix the score for everyone.

Every score has a Contest this finding button. Tell us what you know — your name, your relationship to the product, what you've seen. We verify your email and your report enters our review queue.

When our team validates a correction, the AI score is replaced with a manually-verified one — visible to all future users with a ✓ Verified mark.

It isn't a complaint box. It's a correction mechanism. The whole point is to improve the dataset over time.

FAQ

Common questions.

Which retailers do you support?

Today the extension scores product pages on Amazon (amazon.com, amazon.co.uk, amazon.de, and other regional Amazon sites), Target, and Walmart (walmart.com). eBay and Etsy are on the roadmap after that. If you'd like to tell us which one to build next, get in touch.

Is this only about Chinese products?

No — the engine identifies any country. China detection is the primary calibrated use case, since that's where seller-country ambiguity is most common on large online marketplaces, but the scores work the same way for products made in Vietnam, Mexico, Germany, Korea, or anywhere else. The UI only "lights up" red when there's a high-confidence China score; everything else reads as neutral.

How accurate are the scores?

It varies by signal strength. When the retailer's listing has a structured "Country of Origin" field, we're typically 90–95% confident and well-calibrated. When the field is missing and we're relying on brand recognition or web search, confidence drops appropriately. The confidence band is the most important number on the screen — pay attention to it.

How does Hong Kong / Taiwan get handled?

Hong Kong (HK), Macau (MO), and Taiwan (TW) are scored as their own countries. Never folded into mainland China (CN). This is a hard rule in our system.

Do you collect my browsing history?

No. The extension only activates on product pages of the retailers we support (Amazon, Target, and Walmart), and the only data sent to our backend is the product identifier plus what's visible on that page. We do not collect your retailer account info, your order history, or anything from unsupported sites. Full details in our Privacy Policy.

Is this free?

The first 20 checks per month are free forever, no credit card required. Beyond that, subscriptions start at $4.99/mo — see the pricing page. A portion of every subscription supports human-rights work in China and around the world.

How do I contest a score?

Click the toolbar icon → scroll to Contest this finding → fill in the form. You'll need to provide your name, email, and stated affiliation or credentials. We email you a verification link before the report enters our queue. Verified reports are typically reviewed within a few business days.

I represent a brand — how do I correct a score about my product?

Same path: Contest this finding. We treat first-party brand corrections with high priority. Identify yourself as someone associated with the brand in the "affiliation" field, and provide a corporate email so we can verify the connection.
